A trusted vendor’s keys were used to steal our data and change where we pay a supplier, five days before a payment run

  • Who acted. Between 9 Aug and 6 Sep 2026 a second actor used TallyBridge Connect’s integration credentials (key-tb-07 on LedgerOne, tb-sync-svc on HelpDeskr) alongside the genuine nightly sync. It worked first from home‑broadband addresses (198.18.7.x), then from the vendor’s build server tb-build-02 (10.20.4.77), inside our private link.
  • What it took. It searched support tickets for secrets and took a customer storage key (bvk-7f3a) that had been pasted into ticket TKT‑48213. With that key it read our finance and HR folders. It also exported the vendor list (9,840 records), payments (4,210) and 10 ticket exports, deleting the export jobs afterwards.
  • The fraud. On 3 Sep it changed vendor V‑0412’s bank account (****4417 → ****9083) through the API with no approval, before the 8 Sep run. Nothing in the records shows a past run paid to a changed account.
  • The vendor’s account. TallyBridge’s notices (E12, E16) are contradicted by our logs on all six testable claims.
High confidenceWhat happened, when, from where, with which credentials, and the V‑0412 change.
MediumWho: an outsider who compromised the vendor, or a vendor insider (§5).
UnknownWhether any money left. The bank’s payment files for 23 Aug and 8 Sep would settle it.

  1. Attribution. We attribute by credential and behaviour, not IP address. 198.18.7.0/24 is a dynamic residential range (E20‑G), and staff use it on other days.
  2. Documents. Documents are accurate records of what they state. Facts from E06, E13, E16 and E20 are typed into context.json, each with its source.
  3. Labels. Our labels (analysis/analyst_labels.json) are our judgement, not organiser ground truth. The engine’s scores are measured against them.
  4. Payment files. No bank payment file was supplied, so whether money left can’t be shown either way.

5Conclusions

What happened

The vendor’s integration credentials were long-lived, could write to every module and had no IP restriction (E04). Someone holding them ran a four-week campaign: reconnaissance, theft of secrets from tickets, a pivot into storage with a customer’s key, bulk exports with deletion, and finally a payee bank change made by software with no approval. They worked from the vendor’s own build host inside the private link, where traffic is trusted by default.

Who, and how sure

The actor held TallyBridge’s credentials and could operate tb‑build‑02 after its 16 Aug re‑provisioning. That points to either a vendor insider or an outsider who compromised the vendor’s build or credential systems. E07 notes the vendor’s SOC 2 report excludes its source code and credential vault. We can’t tell which without the vendor’s logs, and E08 §9.1 gives us no right to audit them.

Impact

Data taken: the vendor master, payments, ticket exports, and the bank-details and HR files. Legal notification periods should run from 9 Aug. Money at risk: V‑0412 on 8 Sep. Four other bank changes lack valid approval.

Why it wasn’t caught

No system cross-checked another. The controls existed on paper:

  • audit findings 2–7 were still open (E11);
  • the vendor risk was rated Low and accepted (E09 R‑031);
  • the vendor’s notice went to the billing contact only (E12);
  • the billing alert was treated as an accrual question (E06).

Before the 8 Sep run

  • Hold V‑0412, restore ****4417 and confirm using the phone number in the contract.
  • Verify V‑0162, V‑0128, V‑0233 and V‑0298. Call the contract number, not the one on file.
  • Revoke key-tb-07, tb-sync-svc and bvk-7f3a. Block 10.20.4.77 on the link.
  • Reset a.kapoor’s password. Disable r.menon’s account.
  • Re‑issue the sync key with least privilege: 4 actions, hosts .11–.16, 01:00–02:45 UTC. Replayed on the evidence, this would have refused 133 of 133 attacker requests.

Next 30 days

  • Keep an approval register and take call-back numbers from contracts, not from the vendor record.
  • Hold any bank change made by software until a person approves it.
  • Request TallyBridge’s build and credential-vault logs. Renegotiate audit rights (E08 §9.1).
  • Run Double‑Entry Defense on every log export. It takes about 8 s per run.

What would change our mind

  • An approval record exists for the V‑0412 change.
  • Vendor logs show 10.20.4.77 was used by its staff for an authorised purpose.
  • The bank confirms ****9083 belongs to Coastal Packaging.
